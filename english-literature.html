The periods and their dates

Following the division set out by M. H. Abrams in A Glossary of Literary Terms

Period boundaries are conveniences, not facts. Historians disagree by a decade here or there, and the same stretch of years often carries two names at once — the Shakespearean Age sits inside the Elizabethan, the Puritan Age overlaps the Caroline and the Commonwealth. The dates below are the ones most commonly set in examinations.

#PeriodDatesNamed after
Why the names differ. English literary periods are named in three ways: after a monarch (Elizabethan, Jacobean, Caroline, Victorian, Edwardian), after a dominant writer (the Age of Chaucer, of Dryden, of Pope, of Johnson), or after a movement or historical condition (Renaissance, Restoration, Romantic, Modern). Where a period has two names, an examiner may use either.
